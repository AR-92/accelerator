- Great! Now, let's define the core function of your product. This is the primary purpose or the main feature that your product offers to solve a customer problem. If you're unsure, I can provide some examples to get you started!

  Section 2: Market Section
- Moving on to the market! What category does your product fit into? For example, is it SaaS, a platform, or something else? This helps us understand the industry context.
- Who is your Target Market? You can select multiple if applicable. Think about the types of customers who would benefit most from your product, like e-commerce companies or logistics firms.
- What is your Target Geographic Area? Where do you plan to operate? This could be specific countries, regions, or globally. For example, focusing on the GCC region.
- Let's estimate your TAM, which is the Total Addressable Market. This is the total market size for your product type. For example, the entire logistics market in your region.
- Now, estimate your SAM, the Serviceable Addressable Market. This is the portion of the TAM that you can actually serve with your current capabilities.
- Finally for market size, estimate your SOM, the Serviceable Obtainable Market. This is the realistic market share you can capture in the first few years.
  Section 3: Competition Analysis
- Time to analyze competition! Who are your Key Competitors? List the main players in your market that offer similar solutions.
- What is their Market Share? Estimate how much of the market each competitor holds. This helps us understand the competitive landscape.
- What is the Competition Level in your market? Is it high, medium, or low? This affects your strategy.
- What are your Penetration Opportunities? Where can you find advantages over competitors, like unique features or underserved niches?
- What are the Market Challenges you might face? Things like switching costs, integration issues, or data privacy concerns.
  Section 4: Risk Register
- Now, let's identify your business Risks. What could go wrong? Think about technical issues, market changes, or operational problems.
- Distribute your risks into categories: Technical, Market, and Operational. Estimate the percentage for each to see where most risks lie.
- What's your Overall Risk Level? Based on all the risks, is it low, medium, medium-high, or high?
  Section 5: Value Proposition
- Finally, what is the Customer Job? This is the core problem your customer is trying to solve. What do they want to achieve?
- What are the Customer Pains? These are the problems or frustrations your customers experience that your product can solve.
- What are the Customer Gains? These are the benefits or positive outcomes your customers hope to achieve.
- What are the Solution Components? These are the key features or elements of your product that address the pains and deliver the gains.
  Section 6: Cost & Revenue
- What are your Key Costs? Include fixed and variable costs associated with delivering your product.
- What are your Revenue Streams? How will you make money from your product?

  Idea Model Assistant
  Section 1: Problem Validation
- Please describe in detail the specific problem or challenge that your startup idea aims to address. Include information about who is affected by this problem, how it currently impacts their daily lives or customer operations, the scale of the problem, and why it urgently needs to be solved. Provide concrete examples if possible.
- Identify and describe the primary group of people or organizations who are currently struggling with the problem you described. Provide demographic details, their characteristics, pain points, and how this problem affects their productivity, finances, or quality of life. Be specific about their needs and frustrations.
- List and explain your key assumptions about the problem, the market, and your potential solution. For each assumption, describe why you believe it's true, what evidence you have to support it, and what risks exist if the assumption proves incorrect. This will help validate your understanding of the situation.
- Research and describe the existing solutions or alternatives that people currently use to address this problem. Include both direct competitors and indirect alternatives (workarounds). For each one, explain their strengths, weaknesses, pricing, user adoption, and why they haven't fully solved the problem.
  Section 2: Solution Development
- Provide a clear, compelling title for your proposed solution. This should be concise yet descriptive, capturing the essence of what your product or service does and the value it provides. The title should be memorable and communicate the core benefit to potential users.
- Provide a comprehensive description of your solution, explaining exactly how it works, its key features and capabilities, and how it specifically addresses the problem you identified. Include technical details if relevant, user workflows, and how it differs from existing alternatives. Be detailed enough that someone could understand the solution without prior knowledge.
  Section 3: Customer Model
- Define your ideal target customers in detail. Create detailed customer personas including demographics, psychographics, behaviors, needs, motivations, and pain points. Explain why these specific customers would be most likely to adopt your solution and how your product fits into their current workflows or lifestyles.
- Identify the key performance indicators (KPIs) and metrics that will measure the success of your customer. Include metrics for customer acquisition, retention, revenue, user engagement, and product performance. Explain how you'll track these metrics and what benchmarks indicate success.
- Create a comprehensive list of your direct and indirect competitors. For each competitor, analyze their market position, target customers, pricing strategy, key features, strengths, weaknesses, market share, and growth trajectory. Explain how your solution compares and what your competitive advantages are.
- Articulate your unique value proposition (UVP) - the single most compelling reason why customers should choose your solution over competitors. Clearly state what makes your offering different and better, focusing on specific benefits and outcomes that matter most to your target customers.
- Describe your solution at a high level, focusing on the core concept and how it fundamentally solves the customer's problem. Explain the main idea in simple terms that anyone could understand, avoiding technical jargon. Include the primary mechanism through which value is delivered to users.
- Explain your unfair advantage - the unique assets, capabilities, or positioning that competitors cannot easily replicate. This could include proprietary technology, exclusive partnerships, first-mover advantage, network effects, or other barriers to entry that give you a sustainable competitive edge.
- Describe the channels and distribution strategies you will use to reach your target customers. Include both customer acquisition channels (how people will discover your product) and delivery channels (how they will access and use your solution). Explain your rationale for choosing these channels and how they align with customer preferences.
- Identify your early adopters - the first customers who will try your product and provide feedback. Describe their characteristics, where to find them, what motivates them to try new solutions, and how they differ from your mainstream target customers. Explain how you'll engage with them during the early stages.
- Describe how you will establish and maintain relationships with your customers. Include your customer acquisition strategy, onboarding process, support mechanisms, communication channels, and retention tactics. Explain how you'll build loyalty and encourage long-term engagement with your product or service.
  Section 4: Branding
- Describe your brand identity, including your mission, vision, core values, personality, and positioning in the market. Explain what your brand stands for, how you want customers to perceive it, and how it differentiates you from competitors. Include details about your brand voice, tone, and visual identity elements.
- Craft a compelling brand statement that encapsulates your company's purpose, values, and unique position in the market. This should be a concise, memorable statement that communicates what you do, who you serve, and why you matter. It should inspire both customers and employees.
- Provide your proposed customer name. Explain the reasoning behind your choice, including what the name represents, its memorability, availability (domain, trademarks), and how it aligns with your brand identity and target market. Consider cultural implications and pronunciation.
- Create a catchy, memorable slogan that captures the essence of your brand and value proposition. The slogan should be short, easy to remember, and effectively communicate your key benefit or unique selling point. Explain how it resonates with your target audience.
  Section 5: IP Protection
- Provide a detailed description of your intellectual property protection measures. Explain each type of IP protection you're using, the specific assets protected, filing dates and jurisdictions, protection duration, maintenance requirements, and how you plan to enforce your IP rights. Include any licenses, assignments, or other IP-related agreements.
- Describe your domain name registration strategy. Include the primary domain you've registered, alternative domains you've secured, registration details (registrar, registration date, expiration), and how your domain name strategy supports your branding and online presence. Explain any trademark considerations in domain selection.
- Detail the specific elements of your intellectual property that you're protecting. For each element, explain what it is, why it's valuable, how it's protected (patent, copyright, trademark, etc.), and how it contributes to your competitive advantage. Include both registered and unregistered IP elements.
